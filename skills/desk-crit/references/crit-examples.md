# Crit examples: weak vs strong

Read once if unsure about tone or depth. The works described are fictional.

## 1. Praise

Weak:
> Great job! Love the colors and the overall vibe. Very creative!

Strong:
> **The yellow date is the second thing I read, right after the title.** That's exactly the order a poster needs: what, then when. Keep that color only for the date and the ticket button so it stays meaningful.

Why: names a specific element, explains *why* it works in terms of a principle, and tells the student how to protect it.

## 2. Too many issues vs the right ones

Weak (a list of 14 notes):
> Fonts are inconsistent, the saxophone is clip-art, circles are random, line spacing tight, button has dotted border, exclamation marks, purple stripes too busy, info text small, too many colors, everything centered, lineup font is Times, date has text shadow, margins uneven, no grid.

Strong (beginner, 2 issues):
> **1. Five fonts are fighting** (marker 1) · *Principle: typography*
> Comic, Impact, a script, Times and Arial each say something different, so the poster has no single voice and the eye jumps. Pick one bold display font for the title and one simple sans for everything else.
>
> **2. The background is louder than the words** (marker 2) · *Principle: contrast*
> The diagonal stripes have the same strength as the text, so the small info lines almost vanish. Try a flat background first; add texture back only if the text still reads easily.
>
> Later: once those are fixed, we'll look at alignment.

Why: a beginner can act on two things. The rest is mostly a consequence of these two and will change anyway.

## 3. Escalating a recurring issue

> **3rd time: alignment. Let's fix this for good.** In the dashboard, the KPI cards start at different heights (marker 1) and the table totals change alignment from row to row (marker 2). This is the same thing we saw in the landing page and the poster: elements placed by eye instead of on shared lines. Today's exercise is only about this.

Why: links to history, shows the pattern, not just the instance, and focuses the session. No blame.

## 4. Progress on a new version

> **Progress since v1:** the title is now clearly first and the date second, fixed. You went from 5 fonts to 2, exactly what we discussed. Still open: the info block and the ticket button sit on different baselines and margins (marker 1), so the bottom feels loose compared to the tight top.

Why: specific improvement tied to the previous session, honest about what's still open.

## 5. Calibrating to level

Same issue (secondary text too pale) for three levels:

- **Beginner:** "The grey text under the heading is hard to read: it's too close to the white. Make it darker, around #64748B or darker, and check it with a contrast checker (aim for 4.5:1)."
- **Intermediate:** "Body copy on all three screens is #94A3B8 on white, about 2.6:1. You're using lightness to push it down the hierarchy, but size and position already do that. Darken to ~#475569 (7:1) and let the type scale carry the hierarchy."
- **Advanced:** "You're encoding hierarchy in three channels at once on these screens (size, weight, and a 2.6:1 grey). The third channel costs legibility for no gain. Consider a two-step text token system (primary/secondary at ≥7:1 and ≥4.5:1) and test in sunlight."

## 6. Honest without crushing

Weak: "This doesn't really work."
Weak (inflated): "Almost there, just small tweaks!" (when the structure needs rework)
Strong:
> "Right now the layout doesn't yet tell me what to look at first, which is the main job of a poster. That's a structural fix, not a tweak, but it's the most valuable thing to learn at this stage, and the exercise below takes 30 minutes."

## 7. Respecting the student's intent

> "The rotated square on screen 1 and the soft 3D sphere on screen 2 feel like two different illustration styles. If the idea is that each screen has its own object, great, but give them the same rendering (both flat or both 3D) so the set reads as one product."
