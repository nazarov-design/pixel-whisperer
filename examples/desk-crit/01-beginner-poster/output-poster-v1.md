## Desk crit: Jazz on the Roof poster · beginner

### What works
- **The headline** (marker A). The huge cyan condensed type on dark purple is readable from across a room, which is what a poster needs.
- **A small, bold palette** (marker A). Purple, pink, yellow and cyan feel like a summer night and look cohesive. The big circles add energy without clutter.
- **All the required content is there.** Name, date, lineup and a ticket call-to-action are all present, and the centred axis is consistent throughout.

### Fix first
**1. Everything after the headline is the same quiet volume** (markers 1) · *Principle: hierarchy*
- **What I see:** the date sits at about 30px and the lineup at about 28px, so neither stands out. The info lines are tiny grey-lilac text on purple, which is hard to read. The ticket button is small and says "GET TICKETS NOW!!!" but gives no website, price or venue. The brief said "where to buy tickets", and this doesn't answer it.
- **Why it matters:** someone walking past reads the title, then has no idea when it is or how to go.
- **Try this:** pick a clear second level (date and time), a third (lineup) and a fourth (small print), each visibly different in size. Replace the button text with a real place, for example "Tickets: rooftopclub.com". Drop the "!!!".

**2. Five different type styles** (markers 2) · *Principle: typography*
- **What I see:** a Comic-style script on "presents", a heavy condensed face on the title, a brush script on "Summer Sessions", Times on the lineup, a bold sans on the date, and Arial on the small print.
- **Why it matters:** the fonts compete, so the poster feels like a collage and not one event.
- **Try this:** use one family, or two at most. Keep the bold condensed face for the headline. Pick one clean companion font and get variety from size and weight. The brush script can go, or become the only accent.

**3. Space and shapes aren't working for you** (markers 3) · *Principle: composition*
- **What I see:** all the content is squeezed into the top 75%, and the bottom 230px is empty. The yellow circle cuts into the "DJ Velvet" text. The pink circle sits behind the date, which makes it harder to read. The sax emoji looks like clip art and doesn't match the flat graphic style.
- **Why it matters:** the poster feels top-heavy and unfinished. The shapes should frame the content, not fight it.
- **Try this:** spread the blocks over the full height. Move the circles so they sit behind empty space or off the edges, away from text. Put the tickets at the bottom as an anchor. Later, consider replacing the emoji with a simple flat shape.

### Exercise (40 min): One loud thing
Make 3 versions of the poster. In each, a different single element is the clear focal point: the headline, the date, then the lineup. Everything else gets quieter. Use one font family only.
**Done when:** a friend can point to the focal point in under 2 seconds in each version. Then pick your favourite and write one line on why.

### Study
Look at gig and festival posters from small venues, and Swiss-style event posters. Count how many different type sizes and fonts each one uses. Notice where the date and ticket info sit compared with the title.
