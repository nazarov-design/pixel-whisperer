## Desk crit: Photographer business card, front and back · beginner
(You said you just started, so I judged this as a beginner piece. I assumed the level and didn't ask.)

### What works
- **The script name on the front** (marker A): it's the largest thing on the card and centred, so you read the name first and the job titles second. That order is right for a card.
- **The back has a clear order** (marker A): bold blue studio name, then phone, email, web and address, each on its own line. Someone can find the phone number quickly.
- **One cyan accent colour** carries across both sides, so they look like one set. The white back gives plenty of space for the information.

### Fix first
**1. The front is hard to read** (marker 1) · *Principle: contrast*
- **What I see:** white text on a light gradient, especially toward the green corner. The tagline is thin and small. It also wraps, so "EDITOR" sits alone on a second line, and the line ends in a stray "•".
- **Why it matters:** the front is the first thing people see, and it's the only place that says what your friend does. A printed card will look paler than it does on screen.
- **Try this:**
  - Darken the gradient so white text clearly stands out, or switch to dark text.
  - Shorten the tagline, or put it on two planned lines, e.g. "PHOTOGRAPHER • VIDEOGRAPHER" / "DRONE PILOT • EDITOR".
  - Drop the trailing bullet.

**2. The back's small text is too small and too faint** (marker 2) · *Principle: typography / hierarchy*
- **What I see:** the social handles are tiny light blue, and the quote is tiny light grey italic. On a real card (about 9 cm wide) this would be roughly 5 pt, which is unreadable. Between the contact block and the footer there's a large empty gap. The emoji icons also come from different styles and sizes.
- **Why it matters:** the back exists to be read, and the footer is where people look for your friend's Instagram.
- **Try this:**
  - Set the smallest text to at least 7–8 pt equivalent, in a darker colour.
  - Pick one icon style, or drop the icons and use plain labels.
  - Either cut the quote or give it room.

**3. The card never shows photography** (marker 3) · *Principle: composition*
- **What I see:** a blank blue circle sits where a logo or photo should go, and the front is only a gradient.
- **Why it matters:** a photographer's card is a portfolio piece. Right now it could belong to any business.
- **Try this:**
  - Use your friend's best photo as the full front, or on the back in place of the circle.
  - Keep the name in a darkened or plain area so it stays readable.
  - If it really is a logo placeholder, replace it with the real logo.

Later: the gradient is a very common default look, so a more distinctive colour treatment would help. The script font and the plain sans-serif on the back don't feel related.

### Exercise (30 min): Greyscale version
- **Task:** turn both sides into greyscale (a screenshot with a greyscale filter is enough). Note every piece of text that fades or merges into its background. Fix each one and re-check in greyscale.
- **Done when:** the name, the titles, the phone/email and the handles are all easy to read in greyscale, in the same order of importance as in colour.

### Study
Look at photographers' and studios' business cards, and at minimal card collections. Notice what sits behind the text (a plain area, a dark overlay, white space), how many lines of text there are, and how small the smallest text is compared with the name.
