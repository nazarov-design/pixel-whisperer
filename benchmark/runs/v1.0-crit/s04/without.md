It's a decent start. The layout is clean and the information is easy to find. A few things keep it from looking professional, and they matter more for a photographer, because the card is a sample of their taste.

**What works**
- The back has a clear order: name, then phone, email, web, address.
- It has plenty of white space and isn't cluttered.
- The front and back share one accent blue, so they read as a set.

**What to change, most important first**

1. **The front has no photography.** A photographer's card should show their work. Use one strong photo as the full-bleed front, or on one side of the back. The cyan-to-green gradient looks generic and a bit like a tech startup. It says nothing about this person's style.

2. **The script font on the name is hard to read and looks dated.** It also clashes with the plain sans-serif used everywhere else. Pick one or two typefaces. A clean sans-serif or an elegant serif would be better, and the name should be the biggest element.

3. **The white text on the bright gradient is low contrast.** The tagline especially gets weak on the light green side. If you use a photo, put a dark overlay behind the text or choose a calmer, darker background.

4. **The tagline wraps badly.** "PHOTOGRAPHER • VIDEOGRAPHER • DRONE PILOT • EDITOR" breaks so that "EDITOR" sits alone on a second line. Shorten it, shrink it, or put each role on its own line. Four roles in all caps with bullets is also a lot. Two or three is stronger.

5. **The back has several small-text problems.**
   - The social handles and the quote at the bottom are tiny, and the light gray italic quote is nearly unreadable. Print is less forgiving than a screen, so keep body text at about 7–8 pt or larger.
   - The emoji icons (📞 ✉ 🌐 📍) look amateurish, and they're inconsistent in size. The envelope is much smaller than the others. Use simple line icons in one style, or drop the icons and use plain text.
   - The blue circle is an empty placeholder. Fill it with a logo or photo, or remove it.

6. **The back is unbalanced.** All the content sits in the upper left, with a gap in the middle and small text crammed along the bottom. Align things to a clear grid and group them: contact details together, social links together. Make the margins even. The bottom text almost touches the edge.

7. **The two sides don't feel related.** The front is loud and gradient-heavy, and the back is plain white. Carry something across, such as the same typeface, a color band, or the same photo treatment.

**Before printing**
- Add 3 mm bleed on all sides if the background color or image runs to the edge. Keep important text about 3–5 mm inside the trim.
- Design at 300 dpi at the real card size (for example 85 × 55 mm or 3.5 × 2 in).
- Replace the placeholder text (phone, email, address) and check the QR code or handles actually work.

**A simple direction to try:** a full-bleed photo on the front with the name in a clean typeface and one short line of roles beneath it. The back is white or dark with the logo, three contact lines, and one social handle, all aligned to the same left edge.
