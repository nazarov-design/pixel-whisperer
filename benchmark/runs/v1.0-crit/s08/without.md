Your infographic has a clear structure: headline, key numbers, breakdown, then tips. Here is what I'd improve, most important first.

**1. Fix the layout and empty space**
- The bottom third of the canvas is empty. The content stops at about y=680 on a 1000px canvas. Crop the canvas, or scale the content up to fill it. Right now the piece looks unfinished.
- The title wraps and leaves "GO?" alone on the second line. Shorten it, reduce the size, or break it on purpose ("Where does / our water go?").

**2. Fix the data and its logic**
- The bar chart's 70% "used indoors" stat doesn't connect to the breakdown. Toilet, shower, faucet, washing machine, leaks and "other" add up to 100%, so where's the outdoor 30%? Either label the chart "indoor use" and make it add up to 70%, or add garden and outdoor use. As it stands, a careful reader will see a contradiction.
- "Leaks 12%" next to "1 in 3 homes have a leak" is confusing. One is a share of volume and the other is a share of homes. Say so explicitly, and link the two ideas.
- "Other 8%" is drawn as an empty outline bar. It looks like missing data. Fill it with a neutral grey instead.
- Add a source or a real basis for the figures. The footnote says "Illustrative numbers", which is honest, but it is very small and low in contrast. Keep it, but make it legible.

**3. Improve readability and contrast**
- The footnote is pale blue on pale blue and nearly unreadable. Darken it and enlarge it.
- The big stats (300 L, 70%, 1 in 3) are in a light teal on a light background. Contrast is weak. Use the dark blue from the title.
- The tip text and the bar labels are small (about 12px). For a general audience, aim for 14–16px minimum.
- The bar colors fade from dark to light by rank. That suggests the lighter bars matter less, and it makes the lowest ones faint. The ranking is already shown by bar length, so one solid color would be cleaner. You could highlight just the biggest bar, or the leaks bar, since leaks are your "fixable" story.

**4. Improve the hierarchy and storytelling**
- The three big stats get the same weight, but they aren't equally important. Choose one hero number and make the others secondary.
- Add a takeaway to the chart. For example, "Toilets and showers use 44% of the total", and give the chart its own heading and units.
- Tie the tips to the data. Leaks have a tip, and the shower and the washing machine do too, but the toilet (the largest category) has none. Put each tip near the bar it relates to, or number them in the same order.
- Quantify the tips consistently. "Up to 20 litres" and "20 litres a day" are specific, but the washing-machine tip has no number. Add one.

**5. Polish the visual style**
- The emoji icons are a different visual style from the rest of the piece and render differently on every platform. Replace them with a single consistent set of flat icons in the palette.
- Everything is the same pale blue. White cards on a pale background have very little separation. Add a stronger accent color or a darker band so the sections stand apart.
- Align the content edges. The chart's bars stop at 24% and leave a large empty area on the right, while the cards span the full width. Scaling the bars to the available width would balance it.

**If you only have time for three fixes:** (1) resolve the 70% / 100% inconsistency, (2) darken the light text, and (3) use the empty space at the bottom.
