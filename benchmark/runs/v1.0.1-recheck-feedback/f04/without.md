I've made a round 2 version of the page (`round2.html`, with a preview in `round2.png`). The preview is cut off after the teachers' portraits, so the pricing section isn't visible in it. I haven't looked at that part of the page.

**Why it read as cheap and busy**
- **Colour:** The mint-to-yellow gradient, green bars, orange buttons and orange-outlined price boxes were four colours all competing for attention.
- **Type:** Bold Arial-style text with a drop shadow on a light background looks like a template, and the white text was hard to read.
- **Structure:** Grey cards with empty image boxes, a table with a full grid and a heavy green header, and boxed prices gave every section the same visual weight.

**What I changed**
- **Calm first impression:** The hero is now a warm off-white page with a large serif headline and one short line of copy. It has one muted green button and a tall arched image area on the right. The gradient is gone.
- **Premium feel:** The page uses a restrained palette of warm neutrals, sage and a single clay accent, which appears only in the code and isn't used visibly yet. Headings are in an elegant serif (Cormorant Garamond) with small spaced-out labels above them. Sections have much more space between them.
- **Less busy:** There are no cards, borders or shadows anymore. Sections are separated by space and thin lines, and the prices are plain numbers with a hairline rule.
- **Schedule kept:** The content is unchanged, since the client likes it. It now has thin row lines instead of a grid and a quiet header. The times are in serif, and the empty slots are faded.
- **Teachers added:** A new "Guided by experience" section sits after the schedule, with four arched portrait frames. Each has a name, a specialty and years of teaching. The nav link already existed, and it now has something to point to.

**Before sending to the client**
- **Photos and teachers:** Every image is a placeholder, and the teacher names and years are placeholders too. How calm and premium it feels will depend mostly on real photography: natural light, few people, and no stock-looking poses. The client needs to send the teacher details and portraits.
- **Fonts:** The preview used a fallback serif because the web fonts didn't load offline. The real fonts will look a bit lighter.
- **The "$" prefix:** I added it to the prices, and the original didn't have it. Please confirm the currency.
- **Replies to the client:** I'd tell them that rounds 2 and 3 should be about photography and fine detail, not another change of direction. The brief calls for calm, so I'd resist adding more content to the top of the page.
